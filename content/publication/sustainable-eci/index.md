abstract: "Developing economies are asked to do three things at once: diversify their exports, cut their carbon emissions, and bring unemployment down. The quantitative tools used to design diversification strategies, however, optimize for growth alone. This paper builds a framework that selects export diversification targets subject to a country's climate and employment objectives, both expressed the way a policymaker states them: as reductions to be achieved. A growth equation augmented with the pace of CO2-emission reduction and the pace of unemployment reduction tells us how much economic complexity a country needs to reach a growth target under a given policy scenario; a product-level optimization then finds the cheapest set of new export products that delivers that complexity. Applying the framework to Thailand and Mexico yields three results. First, both margins matter and pull in opposite directions: each percentage point of annual emission reduction has historically been associated with 0.29 points less growth, while each point of unemployment reduction is associated with 0.10 points more, a payoff that rises with complexity. Second, under our central scenario, emissions and unemployment each falling by 2% per year, sustaining 3.5% growth requires more complexity than any attainable export basket can deliver; the best these countries can do is 3.0% (Thailand) and 2.8% (Mexico), and doing so takes an export upgrading program an order of magnitude larger than the growth-only strategy (USD 185 billion versus 19 for Thailand; 243 versus 63 for Mexico). Third, the two commitments price each other almost exactly: across a grid of twelve scenarios, a faster fall in unemployment buys back nearly one-for-one the growth that a faster fall in emissions costs, and whenever the feasibility bound binds, the optimal product list is the same, so the diversification strategy can be committed to even while the policy mix is still being negotiated. The full product lists are reported in the appendix, and a one-command replication package accompanies the paper."
